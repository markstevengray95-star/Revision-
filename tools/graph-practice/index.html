3. Calculating Gradients

The gradient (slope) tells us the rate of change.

How to find a Gradient:

  1. Draw a large right-angled triangle on the line.
  2. Find the change in the y-axis (Δy or "Rise").
  3. Find the change in the x-axis (Δx or "Run").
  4. Divide: Gradient = Δy ÷ Δx.

4. Area Under the Graph

Break complex shapes into simpler rectangles and triangles.

How to find Area under a Graph:

  1. Break the complex shape into simpler parts (rectangles & triangles).
  2. Rectangle Area = base × height
  3. Triangle Area = ½ × base × height
  4. Add the areas together for the total.

5. Categorical Data (Bar Charts)

Learn when to use bar charts and how to construct them correctly.

Key Rules for Bar Charts:

  • Used for Categorical or Discrete data (e.g., categories like colours, types of fuel, or blood groups).
  • Bars must have equal widths.
  • There must be equal gaps between every bar.
  • The Y-axis must have a continuous, evenly spaced scale.
